HDI // Marketing Dashboard

How to use the dashboard.

A plain-English guide for anyone — no marketing background needed. It shows you what this screen is, how to move around it, and what every word means. If you’ve never seen a dashboard like this, start here.

What is this?

It’s one screen that shows how HDI’s marketing and sales are doing — visitors to the website, posts on LinkedIn, blog articles, and the sales pipeline — all in one place, updated automatically from HDI’s real systems. You don’t enter anything. You just open it and read. Think of it like the dashboard in a car: a set of dials that tell you, at a glance, how things are running.

2 · The five tabs — what each one is for

B

Board

“How is the business doing?”
The money view for leadership — how much is in the sales pipeline, how much revenue has been won, and what’s blocking marketing from getting credit for deals.
I

Insights

“What do the website numbers mean?”
The plain-English summary of the website — is traffic growing, how does it compare to the industry, and what’s driving results. Start here if you want the story, not the detail.
W

Website

“Who’s visiting and what do they do?”
The detailed website view — how many people visit, where they come from, which pages they land on, and whether those pages hold their attention.
Bl

Blog

“Which articles are landing?”
A ranked list of blog posts by how much traffic they draw — so you can see which topics resonate and which don’t. Every post is clickable.
Li

LinkedIn

“Is our brand growing?”
How HDI’s LinkedIn page is performing — followers over time, how posts perform, and which kinds of posts get the most engagement.
There’s also a Benchmarks tab (the industry standards the team compares against) — useful, but not one of the five you’ll live in day-to-day.

3 · How to read any number on the screen

↑ +48%
A green up-arrow = better than the period before. This is good.
↓ −12%
A red down-arrow = worse than the period before. Worth a look.
bar
A lime bar = it clears the industry benchmark (a good result).
bar
A lighter bar = below the benchmark. Context, not alarm.
—
A dash = no data yet. Never a zero. Nothing is invented.
was 32%
“was …” = the value in the previous period, so you can see the change.
“vs prior period” means the dashboard compares your chosen window to the one right before it — e.g. the last 30 days against the 30 days before that. Same length, fair comparison. “Reference” next to a benchmark means it’s an industry figure shown for context, not yet an official HDI goal.

4 · Words you’ll see, in plain English

Visitor — one person who came to the website.
Session — one visit. One person can visit several times.
Impression — one time a post was shown to someone (LinkedIn).
Engagement rate — the share of visits/posts where people actually did something (read, clicked, reacted) — a quality score.
Channel — how someone arrived: a Google search, a direct visit, a social link, etc.
Landing page — the first page someone lands on when they arrive.
Bot — automated (non-human) web traffic. About half of all web traffic is bots — normal, and filtered out where it matters.
Pipeline — the total value of sales deals currently in progress.
Closed won — deals that were signed and became revenue.
Followers — people who subscribed to HDI’s LinkedIn page.
Benchmark — the typical industry number, shown so you know if HDI is ahead or behind.
GA4 / Clarity — the two behind-the-scenes tools that measure the website. You never open them — the dashboard reads them for you.

5 · Try it — your first 60 seconds

  1. Open the dashboard link. You’ll land on the Board tab.
  2. At the top, click 30D to look at the last 30 days.
  3. Click the Website tab. Read the top numbers — visitors, sessions — and their green/red arrows.
  4. Scroll to Landing pages and Top pages. Click any page name to open the real page.
  5. Click the LinkedIn tab and look at the Followers over time line — is it climbing?
  6. That’s it. Nothing you click changes any data — explore freely.
The one promise behind every number: everything you see is read live from HDI’s real systems and stamped with where it came from. Nothing is estimated, rounded up, or filled in — if a number isn’t known yet, it shows a dash. You can trust what’s on the screen.
